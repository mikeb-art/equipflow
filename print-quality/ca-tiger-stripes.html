Small numbers, flat

CA sees 3–23 tiger-stripe reprints a month, as many in the 45–50% winter as in the 58–60% summer. There is no seasonal pattern to explain.

Bursts, not weather

When CA spikes it is one press in one week — 16 in the week of Mar 9, 16 of 17 on the P5 Tex‑1 in the week of Aug 31. With material that is stable roll to roll, those point at a calender setting or an operator setup, not at room humidity, which changes slowly and hits everything at once.

Best theory and what to do

Theory

CA has no chronic tiger-stripe problem — 3 to 23 a month, the same in a 45% winter as a 60% summer — it has two burst weeks that make half the year's total. A burst on one press in one week (16 of 17 on the P5 Tex‑1 in the week of Aug 31), with stable material, is a setup that was wrong for a few days: a tension or pressure setting changed, a felt not tracking, or a paper-to-fabric alignment that one operator got wrong and then corrected. Environment is not the driver here; at most it makes a marginal setting show up sooner.

Suggestions

1. Trace the two burst weeks: who ran the calender, which Monti, and what changed the week before (the CA Monti actuator and inverter work fell in May–July). 2. Post the settings sheet on both Montis and make a start-of-shift check the rule, as at MX. 3. Stop typing tiger stripes under "other" — 24 of CA's 92 summer events were free text — and record the sublimator on the reprint, so a burst can be pinned to a machine and a shift the same week it happens. 4. If a burst repeats, split one roll across Monti 1 and Monti 2 the same day to confirm it is the machine and not the roll.
