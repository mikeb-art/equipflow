What it shows

Tiger stripes were worst in the driest months — and those were also the months both PA Montis had documented take-up and felt-tracking problems. The two moved together, so this year's data can't say which one caused it.

What would settle it

Next winter with healthy calenders. If the stripes stay low when RH drops to the 20s, it was the machines; if they come back, it's the dry air. Recording the sublimator on every reprint (two-thirds blank this winter) makes the split clean.

Best theory and what to do

Theory

PA's winter spike was the calenders, with dry air making it worse. Monti 2's take-up drive was failing from November and Monti 1's felt was tracking off from January; where a sublimator was recorded, Monti 2 accounts for 64 of the 81. Uneven felt tension and a hunting take-up produce exactly this defect — bands across the web where paper and fabric slip against each other in the nip — and a machine that is marginal will show it first when the paper is dry and static-charged at 20% RH. Material is stable and bought by the container, so lot variation is not a candidate. The reprints fell within two weeks of the March service and stayed low through a wet summer, which is what you would expect if the machine was the cause and humidity only the amplifier.

Suggestions

1. Before winter: full tension check on both Montis — piston balance left/right, felt stretch measured and recorded, take-up tension calibrated — and a written settings sheet per machine. 2. Bring the Monti area up to at least 35–40% RH in January–March (a print-room humidifier is cheap against 200 reprints) and keep the printed paper wrapped until it goes on the calender. 3. Record the sublimator on every reprint (155 of 236 winter events have it blank) so next winter the two machines can be told apart week by week. 4. If stripes return this winter on serviced machines, that is the humidity signal — then split one roll across Monti 1 and Monti 2 the same day to confirm it is not one machine again.
