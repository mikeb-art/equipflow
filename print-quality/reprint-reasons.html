"Graphic not received": where did it go missing?

For each of the 594 graphic-not-received reprints since April, the scans that line already had before the reprint was filed. If it was never scanned Printed, it was a queue or nesting miss at the press. If it was Printed but never Sublimated, it was lost between press and calender. If it had both, the line was signed off through print and sublimation and still came up short in finishing.

never printedprinted, not sublimatedprinted and sublimated

What it means

85–91% of these lines had been scanned through both print and sublimation before finishing reported them missing. Only 2–3% were never printed at all. So this is not a queue miss at the press — it is a line that was marked complete on a roll when a piece of it was not there, or a piece that separated between the calender and the finishing table. Both are operator errors, but at the scan and the hand-off, not at the RIP. The missing pieces also cluster by roll — at PA in September, one roll accounts for four different orders reported short, three other rolls for three or four each — so it is the tail of a roll going missing, not single pieces. A piece count at the Printed scan against the line quantity, and a code that says "short on roll" with a count instead of "graphic not received", would make this traceable the same day.

Trend

PA had 8, 7 and 8 a month through June–August and then 80 in September (58 through both scans, 19 printed-only); MX rose from 37 to 83 over the same stretch; CA is steady at 12–20. At MX 58% are filed on nights. Almost all of PA's "printed, not sublimated" cases are Super Flat Vinyl off the P5-350, which never goes to a Monti, so for PA that bucket is really "printed, then short at finishing" too. The reprints are filed by QC, cutters and Zund operators at PA, by two production operators at MX, and mostly by one lead at CA; they ask for part of the line, not the whole line — 314 pieces across 179 PA reprints on lines totalling 409, 568 across 312 at MX on lines totalling 6,056 — which fits pieces missing from a roll rather than whole jobs lost.

Checked against the Durst print logs — CA (July–September) and PA (September)

Every Durst logs each job with the SO number, copies requested, copies done and final state — the CA presses via their PrintSequences files, the PA presses via the Durst Analytics+ server. Matching the graphic-not-received reprints against those logs, CA's 59 since July and PA's 80 in September:

A large share never printed

CA: 22 of the 45 checkable reprints had no job on the press until after the reprint was filed. PA: 32 of 80. In both plants the reprint job appears within an hour or two of the reprint being filed, and there is no earlier job for that SO anywhere near the time PSU says it was printed. The presses name every job by SO (CA's 325 has only test targets and gaps unnamed; PA's 325s name jobs by SO and SKU), so it isn't hiding in a nest. Yet PSU has a Printed scan for every one of them. The clearest case is PA roll 6353: four different orders scanned Printed on the 325-1 at 18:55 on Sep 10, all four reported missing on Sep 14, and the press has no record of any of them until the reprints ran that afternoon. The roll was scanned; it was never printed.

The rest printed complete

CA: 23 of 45 had the job Done with the full copy count before the reprint was filed. PA: 46 of 80 (a handful matched on another SKU of the same order, so call it 40–46). Those pieces existed on a roll, went through the Monti, and were lost in finishing. Press-side shortfalls are rare and are handled at the press: at PA the P5-350 errored mid-run on three multi-piece vinyl jobs in September and re-ran the balance the same day; only one of those became a graphic-not-received reprint.

So the code covers two different failures: a press-side miss that the Printed scan should have caught — 40% of PA's and half of CA's — and a finishing loss after a complete print. The first is fixed at the scan (count pieces against the line, scan from the roll rather than the queue); the second is fixed at the Monti hand-off and the cutting table. MX can be checked the same way from its Analytics+ entries.
